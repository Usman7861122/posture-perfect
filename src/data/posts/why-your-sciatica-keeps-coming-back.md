One of the most common reasons people look for a chiropractor in Dallas is sciatica. And honestly, people suffering from sciatica are miserable. The pain can be relentless.

For some people it feels like:

- burning down the leg
- electric shocks
- numbness
- tingling
- weakness
- cramping
- deep aching
- sharp pain every time they stand up

Some people cannot sit comfortably, drive, sleep, work out, bend forward, walk normally, pick up their kids, or even put on their shoes without pain.

One of the biggest frustrations patients have is this: “Why does it keep coming back?” That’s the real question. Many people temporarily calm sciatica without ever fixing why it developed in the first place.

## Sciatica is often more than just a tight muscle

One of the biggest misconceptions online is that sciatica is always a tight piriformis, a muscle spasm, or something you can stretch away in two days. Sometimes it is muscular. But many cases involve disc injuries, spinal instability, inflammation, nerve irritation, poor posture, repetitive stress, movement dysfunction, degeneration, muscle imbalance and sedentary lifestyles.

Sciatica is not actually a diagnosis itself. It’s a symptom. The sciatic nerve is being irritated somewhere along its pathway. The real question is: why?

## What actually causes sciatica?

Some of the most common causes include:

- herniated discs
- bulging discs
- spinal stenosis
- inflammation
- degenerative changes
- poor biomechanics
- instability
- repetitive lifting
- prolonged sitting
- weak stabilizing muscles
- postural stress

Modern life is brutal on the lower back. People spend hours driving, sitting at desks, and staring at phones. The body adapts to those positions. Eventually muscles weaken, hips tighten, posture changes, discs overload, and nerves become irritated. Then one day the leg starts burning.

## Why sciatica keeps returning

This is where many people get stuck. They stretch temporarily, take medications, rest for a few days, get a massage, watch videos, or use heating pads. The symptoms calm down, but the dysfunction underneath remains. That’s why the pain often comes back.

Long-term improvement usually requires more than symptom suppression.

## The sitting problem nobody talks about

One of the biggest hidden contributors to sciatica today is prolonged sitting. When people sit excessively, hip flexors tighten, glutes weaken, core stability declines, lumbar discs experience prolonged pressure, and posture collapses. Then people stand up quickly, twist poorly, or lift something awkwardly, and suddenly the nerve flares up.

The body was designed to move. Not sit all day.

This frustrates many patients. Some people have terrible MRI findings with minimal symptoms. Others have severe pain with “mild” imaging findings. That’s because imaging is only part of the story. The body is dynamic.

## Why inflammation matters

Sciatica is not always purely mechanical. Inflammation plays a major role. Things that may worsen inflammation include poor sleep, obesity, processed foods, blood sugar instability, sedentary lifestyles, chronic stress, smoking and metabolic dysfunction.

This is one reason functional health matters. The healthier the body becomes overall, the better it often heals and recovers.

## The goal is not just temporary relief

Anybody can chase symptoms temporarily. But real recovery often means rebuilding posture, movement, stability, flexibility, strength and lifestyle habits. That is where long-term results happen.

## When sciatica becomes serious

You should seek immediate medical evaluation if you develop:

- severe weakness
- loss of bowel or bladder control
- progressive numbness
- inability to walk normally
- rapidly worsening neurological symptoms

Those situations require urgent evaluation.
